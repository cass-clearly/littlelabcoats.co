# Little Lab Coats Funnel Plan

_Date: 2026-05-31_

## Goal
Build a Hook Point-style conversion funnel for Little Lab Coats **without changing the current homepage first**.

This borrows the **structure** that Hook Point uses well:
- strong hook above the fold
- immediate pain/agitation
- proof and clarity
- single focused CTA
- a clean next step

For LLC, the correct CTA is **not** a strategy call.
It should be:
- **Get a free sample lesson**
- then **join the email list**
- then **buy a unit bundle or full-grade bundle**

---

## Recommended funnel architecture

### 1. Traffic source
Primary top-of-funnel sources:
- Instagram Reels
- Facebook posts / reels
- Pinterest pins
- parenting / homeschool sharing groups
- optional short-form ads later

### 2. Dedicated landing page
Purpose:
- convert cold traffic into email leads
- sell the promise of easy, real, hands-on science
- focus on one action only

Primary CTA:
- **Download the free lesson**

### 3. Opt-in / delivery step
Options:
- simplest: email form + instant redirect to thank-you/download page
- alternate: direct browser access plus email capture for bonus materials

Best lead magnet format:
- one **high-conviction free lesson** that proves the brand fast
- household supplies only
- colorful printable
- clear parent guidance
- multi-age friendly if possible

### 4. Thank-you page
Purpose:
- deliver the freebie
- set expectation for emails
- offer the natural next product

Suggested secondary CTA:
- **Browse unit bundles**
- or **See the full science collection**

### 5. Email nurture sequence
Purpose:
- move from curiosity to trust to purchase
- highlight ease, credibility, and actual learning outcomes

### 6. Sales destination
Best first offer:
- a single, easy-to-understand unit bundle

Best second offer:
- full-grade bundle

## Core positioning

### The promise
Little Lab Coats helps homeschool families do **real science fast** using household supplies, beautiful printables, and open-and-go lesson plans.

### The problem it solves
Parents are tired of:
- piecing science together from random sources
- using screen-heavy science content
- buying weird supplies
- adapting classroom material for home
- doing all that work and still not feeling confident

### The emotional outcome
- “I can actually do science this week.”
- “My kids were engaged immediately.”
- “This felt doable, fun, and real.”
- “I didn’t have to overprepare.”

## Funnel offer recommendation

### Best first lead magnet
**Free sample lesson funnel**

Recommended angle:
- “Open-and-go homeschool science lesson”
- “Household supplies only”
- “No screens required”
- “Built by a homeschooling PhD scientist”

Best candidate types:
1. a kindergarten wow lesson
2. a mixed-age crowd-pleaser
3. a lesson with an unusually strong visual result

My recommendation:
- use a **kindergarten or early-elementary ‘instant win’ lesson** as the main lead magnet
- it will likely convert best because it feels easy, colorful, and low-friction

## Suggested funnel page sections

### Section 1: Hero
Objective:
- stop the scroll
- communicate the transformation fast
- push one CTA

Must communicate:
- easy
- hands-on
- homeschool-specific
- low prep
- real science

### Section 2: The problem
Show the pain clearly:
- too much prep
- too many scattered resources
- not enough confidence
- content that looks educational but is actually passive

### Section 3: The better way
Introduce LLC as the simpler system:
- print
- gather household items
- do the experiment
- talk through the science

### Section 4: What’s included
Show what families get:
- experiment
- parent-friendly science background
- worksheet
- discussion prompts
- answer key
- reference card / quiz where relevant

### Section 5: Why parents trust it
Proof points:
- built by a homeschooling PhD scientist
- designed for K–5 homeschool families
- multi-age friendly
- no specialty materials
- beautiful, usable format

### Section 6: Featured sample
Make the free lesson feel tangible.
Show:
- what kids will do
- what supplies they need
- what they’ll learn
- how long it takes

### Section 7: CTA repeat
Repeat the main ask clearly.

### Section 8: Soft next step after opt-in
After the free lesson, direct families to:
- starter unit bundle
- best-selling unit
- grade collection

## Product path recommendation

### Best simple funnel path
1. Free sample lesson
2. Thank-you page with featured unit bundle
3. Email sequence
4. Unit bundle purchase
5. Later upsell to full-grade bundle

### Why this is the best starting path
It matches how homeschool buyers usually behave:
- try first
- trust second
- buy once they can picture themselves using it

---

## What not to copy from Hook Point
Do **not** copy:
- aggressive agency-style scarcity language
- strategy-call-first sales flow
- big-ego performance claims that don’t fit curriculum
- overly masculine / hype-heavy direct response tone

For LLC, the stronger fit is:
- warm confidence
- practical clarity
- visible parent empathy
- proof through usefulness

---

## Low-risk implementation plan

### Phase 1: Draft only
- create landing page copy
- create thank-you page copy
- create email sequence copy
- choose free lesson CTA

### Phase 2: Add as separate pages
Suggested paths:
- `/free-science-lesson/`
- `/thanks/free-science-lesson/`

This leaves the current homepage intact.

### Phase 3: Send traffic
- reels
- pins
- organic posts
- optional ads later

### Phase 4: Measure
Track:
- landing page conversion rate
- email signup rate
- clickthrough to shop
- first purchase rate

---

## Recommendation
If only one funnel gets built first, build:

**Free lesson landing page → thank-you page → 4-email welcome sequence → featured unit bundle offer**

That is the cleanest, safest, most LLC-native version of the Hook Point model.
