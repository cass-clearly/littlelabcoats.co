Erosion Engineer

Earth Systems · Water, sediment & landscapes · 55–75 min · NGSS MS-ESS2-2

Mission: Model a rainstorm and investigate: How does ground cover change the amount or pattern of sediment moved by water?

Science you need

Weathering breaks material down; erosion transports it; deposition occurs when moving water loses energy and drops sediment. A model cannot be a landscape, but it can isolate a cause-and-effect relationship. Vegetation can intercept rain, slow runoff, and hold soil with roots.

Safety & materials: shallow tray, potting soil/sand, measuring cup, water, ruler, pebbles/grass clippings/fabric as cover, collection cups. Work over a tray; do not pour soil down drains.

Design your enquiry

Choose your evidence

Will you measure cloudy runoff volume, collected sediment mass, channel width, changed slope shape, or a ranked photo scale? Explain why your measure fits the question.

Controls to defend

Keep tray angle, soil amount, water volume, pour height, and storm duration equal. Compare bare soil against one or more cover treatments; run repeated “storms.”

Hypothesis: If __________________ covers the soil, then __________________ because __________________.

Make the case

Use a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph to recommend a ground-cover strategy. State one way your tray model differs from a watershed and why that limits your conclusion.

Stretch your thinking

Test an interaction: does ground cover work differently on a steeper slope or during a larger “storm”? Distinguish reduced erosion from increased deposition. Use a mass balance: where did sediment leave, travel, and end up?
