Friction Track

Physical Science · Forces & motion · 50–70 min · NGSS MS-PS2-2 / MS-ETS1-3

Mission: Build a fair test that answers: How does the surface after a ramp affect the distance a toy car travels?
IXL: forcesvariablesdata & graphs

Science you need

Friction is a contact force that opposes sliding or rolling motion. Different surfaces create different amounts of friction. A force can change an object's speed or direction; your measured travel distance is evidence about the net effect of the forces acting after release.

Safety & materials: toy car, ramp/book, ruler or tape measure, tape, 3–4 surfaces (cardboard, felt, foil, sandpaper), pencil. Keep ramps low and the runway clear.

Design your enquiry

Decide before testing
  • Independent variable: __________________
  • Dependent variable: __________________
  • At least 3 controls: __________________
  • Number of trials per surface: ______
Fair-test constraints

Use one car, the same ramp height and release line, same runway length and the same measurement point. You choose the surface set and explain why it is a useful comparison.

Write your hypothesis: If __________________, then __________________ because __________________.

Evidence notebook

Graph it: Make a bar graph of mean distance (y-axis) versus surface (x-axis).

Make the case

Claim: Which surface produced the greatest/least friction? Evidence: use numerical means. Reasoning: explain how friction changes motion. Then name one source of uncertainty and one design improvement.

Stretch your thinking

Draw a free-body diagram for the car on the flat track: gravity, support force, motion direction, and friction. Why is travel distance an indirect measure rather than a direct measurement of friction force? Design a version that uses a spring scale to measure a pulling force instead.
