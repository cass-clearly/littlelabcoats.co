Potato Osmosis

Life Science · Cells & solutions · 24–48 hours · NGSS MS-LS1-2

Mission: Investigate: How does solution concentration affect potato tissue? You decide whether mass, length, firmness, or more than one measurement best answers it.

Science you need

Plant cells have selectively permeable membranes. Water moves across them by osmosis: net movement goes toward the side with a higher concentration of dissolved particles. Cells that gain water become more turgid; cells that lose water become less firm. The potato is a model of many plant cells—not one giant cell.

Safety & materials: adult slices potato into equal strips; 3–4 cups, water, salt or sugar, measuring spoon, ruler and/or kitchen scale, labels. Do not eat experiment materials. Wash hands and discard solutions afterward.

Design your enquiry

Plan choices

Constraint

Use the same liquid volume and soak time for every cup. Photograph or measure strips before and after. Use at least two strips per condition if possible; cells are messy little citizens.

Stretch your thinking

Explain why a solution can be more concentrated even when it contains the same amount of water. Predict what will happen at an isotonic concentration. Calculate percent change in mass: (after − before) ÷ before × 100. What does that improve over a raw-mass comparison?
