Ice Rescue Insulation Challenge

Physical Science · Thermal energy · 45–60 min · NGSS MS-PS3-3

Mission: Find out: Which insulating material or design best slows energy transfer into an ice cube? You set the comparison—but make it fair.

Science you need

Thermal energy moves from warmer to cooler matter. Conduction transfers energy through contact; convection transfers it through moving fluids such as air; radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves. Insulators slow transfer, often by trapping still air. “Cold” does not flow out of an ice cube: energy flows in.

Safety & materials: identical cups, identical ice cubes, timer, ruler/spoon for meltwater, materials such as fabric, paper, foil, bubble wrap, and tape. Keep water away from electronics. An adult should handle any cutting tool.

Design your enquiry

Make it a fair comparison

Use equal ice mass/size, the same cup shape, same location and time, and one clearly defined material or design difference. Include an uninsulated control.

Pick an outcome

Measure meltwater volume, remaining ice mass, or temperature at timed intervals. Decide why your measure is valid before the timer starts.

Make the case

Use a CER to name the most effective design. Explain which transfer pathways it likely slowed and identify a confounding variable in your setup.

Stretch your thinking

Why might shiny foil help in some setups but not others? Compare a single layer with several air-trapping layers. Propose a design that reduces conduction, convection, and radiation at once—and explain trade-offs such as material cost and thickness.
