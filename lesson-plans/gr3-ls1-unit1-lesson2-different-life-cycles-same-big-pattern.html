Tracking Change Through One Life Cycle

Life Science — Life Cycles | Unit 1, Lesson 2 of 6

📚 Grade: Grade 3⏱️ Duration: 45–60 minutes🔬 Subject: Life Science
From broad pattern to evidence

In Lesson 1, students learned that all life cycles share the same big pattern: birth, growth, reproduction, and death. In this lesson, they track one organism across time and decide when the organism is still the same individual changing and when a new generation begins.

The key idea: growth changes one organism over time, but reproduction starts a new organism and keeps the cycle going.
📋 NGSS Standard:
3-LS1-1 — Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles but all have in common birth, growth, reproduction, and death.

The Big Idea

One organism can change a lot as it grows, but it is still the same organism. A life cycle becomes a cycle when an adult reproduces and a new young organism begins the pattern again.

The Science

A life cycle is the order of stages in an organism’s life. Some organisms hatch from eggs, some begin as seeds, and some are born live. Some go through big body changes, like metamorphosis, and others grow more gradually. Even with those differences, scientists can still track the same four parts in every life cycle: birth, growth, reproduction, and death.

Follow one frog across time

Important distinction: egg → tadpole → froglet → adult is one organism changing over time. The new eggs are not the same frog changing again. They are the next generation.

Let's Get Started

Step 1: Review the pattern. Say the four parts together: birth, growth, reproduction, and death. Remind students that Lesson 1 introduced the pattern across many organisms.
Step 2: Track one organism. Follow the frog timeline and ask which boxes show the same frog growing and which box shows a brand-new frog beginning life.
Step 3: Compare with other organisms. Talk through a butterfly, a bean plant, a bird, and a human. The stage names differ, but each one still shows a beginning, growth, reproduction, and an end of life.
Step 4: Sort change vs. new generation. Ask students to decide whether a stage card belongs in the “same organism changing” group or the “new generation begins” group.
Step 5: Use the worksheet. Students order one life cycle, compare two organisms, and explain why reproduction is the part that makes the pattern a cycle.

Discussion Questions

Why is a tadpole and an adult frog still the same organism?Hint: think about one living thing changing over time instead of a brand-new frog appearing.
What part of the life cycle starts a new generation?Hint: look for the moment when offspring begin life.
How can a bean plant and a bird have different stage names but the same big pattern?Hint: the names differ, but both still begin life, grow, reproduce, and die.
Why do scientists still call it a cycle if one organism eventually dies?Hint: the next generation keeps the pattern going.

Extensions

  • Choose one more organism and name which stages show growth versus the beginning of a new generation.
  • Make a mini poster that shows one organism changing across time with arrows.
  • Add labels for birth, growth, reproduction, and death to a shark, chicken, or oak tree life cycle.

Parent/Teacher Notes

Common misconceptions to watch for:
  • Students may think every new stage means a new organism. Keep pointing back to the idea that one organism changes as it grows.
  • Students may think reproduction and growth are the same. Clarify that growth changes the same organism, while reproduction starts offspring.
  • Students may think death makes the cycle stop forever. Reconnect them to reproduction and a new generation beginning.
  • Accept equivalent stage labels when the science is right.

🔑 PARENT ANSWER KEY — Detach before giving worksheet to students

1️⃣ Put One Life Cycle in Order — Sample acceptable answers

  • Butterfly: egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → adult butterfly → new eggs
  • Frog: eggs → tadpole → froglet → adult frog → new eggs
  • Bean plant: seed → seedling → young plant → mature plant → new seeds
  • Bird: egg → chick → young bird → adult bird → new eggs
  • Human: baby → child → teenager → adult → new baby begins the next generation

2️⃣ Same Organism or New Generation? — Sample ideas

Same organism changing: tadpole and froglet, or seedling and young plant.
How do you know? It is the same living thing growing and changing over time.
New generation begins: new eggs, new seeds, or a new baby.

3️⃣ Compare Two Organisms — Sample ideas

Same: both begin life, grow, reproduce, and die.
Different: one may hatch from an egg or go through metamorphosis while the other is born live or grows more gradually.

4️⃣ Show the Big Pattern — Accepted answers

All life cycles include birth, growth, reproduction, and death.

5️⃣ Explain Why It Is a Cycle — Model response

“A frog changes from egg to tadpole to froglet to adult, but it is still the same frog growing. When the adult reproduces and new eggs are laid, a new generation begins. That is why it is called a cycle.”

✅ What Success Looks Like

  • Student can correctly order one organism’s stages.
  • Student can tell the difference between one organism growing and a new generation beginning.
  • Student can compare two organisms using evidence, not just names.
  • Student can explain why reproduction makes the pattern a cycle.

🛠️ Troubleshooting / Teaching Notes

  • If a student says every new stage is a new organism, ask: “Did a brand-new frog appear, or did the same frog change?”
  • If a student skips reproduction, ask what happens when an organism becomes an adult.
  • If a student says plants are different because they do not move, bring them back to the life-cycle pattern instead of behavior.
