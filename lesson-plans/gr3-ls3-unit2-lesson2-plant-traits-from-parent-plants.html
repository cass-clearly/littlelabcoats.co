Plant Traits From Parent Plants

Life Science — Traits & Inheritance | Unit 2, Lesson 2 of 6

Grade: 3Duration: 40–50 minutesSubject: Life Science
Plants inherit visible family traits.

Leaf shape, flower color, stem type, and fruit features can repeat from parent plants to offspring. Small differences are variation, not evidence that the plants are unrelated.

NGSS 3-LS3-1: Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence that plants have traits inherited from parents and that variation exists among similar organisms.

Learning Objective

Students will match three offspring plants to likely parent plants, sort by observable traits, and defend each match with two pieces of visual evidence.

The Science

Observe structures.
Look at leaf outline, leaf edge, stem growth, flower shape and color, pods, and fruit.
Use more than one clue.
A match based on two repeated traits is stronger than a match based only on size or color.
Expect variation.
Related plants can differ in height, number of leaves, or exact shade while keeping a family pattern.

Parent–Offspring Plant Visuals

Use these supplied visuals before cutting the challenge cards. Students point to or circle two shared traits in each family and one visible difference.

Sunflower family: compare petals, centers, leaves, and height.
Pea family: compare vines, leaf shape, pods, and size.

Materials

  • Printed plant cards and sorting mat from this lesson
  • Scissors, pencil, and glue stick or reusable tape
  • Optional real leaves for a final comparison

Hands-On Investigation: Find the Plant Families

Challenge: Cut out and mix all six cards. Match each offspring to its likely parent. A match counts only when the student names two visible traits that support it.
Step 1 — Build a trait key (5 min).
Review the picture key: heart-shaped leaf, narrow leaf, toothed leaf edge, flower color, pod, and fruit.
Step 2 — Cut and inspect (8 min).
Cut out the six cards. On each card, underline or circle two useful visual clues.
Step 3 — Match families (10 min).
Place one parent and one offspring in each sorting-mat column. Do not use size alone because offspring plants are often smaller.
Step 4 — Test the matches (8 min).
Swap one offspring card into the wrong family. Ask the student to identify which traits no longer match and repair the sort.
Step 5 — Record evidence (10 min).
Complete the data table and write one claim about inherited plant traits and variation.

Claim

The offspring card _____ matches parent card _____ because both have __________________ and __________________.

They differ in __________________, showing variation within a plant family.

Parent/Teacher Notes

Do not reveal the letters as pairs. Let students rely on structures. If they match by color alone, ask for a second clue. Remind them that the pictures show offspring at a younger stage, so height is weak evidence.

Parent Answer Key

A + F: bean family—heart-shaped leaves, purple flowers, green pods. B + D: tomato family—toothed leaves, yellow flowers, red fruit. C + E: corn family—long narrow leaves, single tall stem, yellow ear.

Sample claim: “Card F matches Card A because both have heart-shaped leaves and purple flowers. Card F is shorter, showing variation and a difference in age.”
